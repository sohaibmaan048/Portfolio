# Looking Back at Programming Fundamentals: More Than Just Learning Python

When I started my first semester as a Computer Engineering student, Programming Fundamentals was one of the subjects that attracted me the most. At the same time, it was also one of the subjects that made me nervous. Before joining university, I had heard many stories about programming. Some people described it as exciting, while others made it sound extremely difficult. Since I had very little programming experience, I was not sure what to expect.

Now that the semester has ended, I can confidently say that Programming Fundamentals became one of the most important courses of my first year. The course taught me Python, but more importantly, it taught me how to think differently when approaching problems.

During the first few weeks of the semester, everything felt new. I was adjusting to university life, managing multiple subjects, attending labs, and trying to understand concepts that were completely unfamiliar. Writing even a small Python program seemed challenging. I remember spending a lot of time trying to understand simple syntax rules and wondering why my programs were producing errors.

At that stage, I believed programming was mainly about writing code. I thought that if I memorized enough commands and keywords, I would become a good programmer. However, as the semester progressed, I discovered that programming is much more about logic than memorization.

The first major change came when we started solving programming problems independently. Instead of following examples from lectures, I had to think about how a problem should be solved before writing any code. This process was difficult because there was no single formula that worked for every situation. Each problem required analysis, planning, and logical thinking.

There were many occasions when I felt stuck. Sometimes I would spend a long time trying to understand why a program was not working. Other times I would write code that looked correct but produced completely unexpected results. These moments were frustrating, especially when deadlines were approaching and multiple assignments from different subjects were competing for attention.

One thing I learned during the semester is that programming requires patience. Unlike some subjects where memorization can help you pass exams, programming demands practice and persistence. Every mistake becomes an opportunity to learn something new.

The debugging process was particularly important for me. In the beginning, every error message felt like a problem. By the end of the semester, I started seeing errors as clues that could help me identify mistakes. This shift in mindset made programming less stressful and much more enjoyable.

The ATM project was one of the highlights of the course. It was the first time I felt that I was building something meaningful rather than simply completing exercises. Although the project was relatively small, it allowed me to combine variables, conditions, loops, and problem solving techniques into a complete application.

Completing that project gave me confidence. For the first time, I felt that I could take an idea and turn it into a working program. That feeling was very different from solving individual lab exercises. It made me realize how much progress I had made since the first week of the semester.

Another valuable lesson from Programming Fundamentals was learning how to approach complex problems. Before university, I often tried to solve problems all at once. Programming taught me to divide challenges into smaller pieces. Instead of focusing on the entire problem, I learned to solve one part at a time.

This approach helped me not only in programming but also in other academic subjects. Whether working on assignments, preparing for quizzes, or studying for exams, I found myself applying the same structured thinking process that I had developed while coding.

As the semester continued, I also became curious about the broader world of technology. Learning Python introduced me to topics such as automation, data analysis, artificial intelligence, and machine learning. Although these areas were beyond the scope of the course, they motivated me to continue learning outside the classroom.

One idea that was frequently emphasized throughout the semester was the importance of using technology to solve real world problems. This perspective helped me understand why programming is such a valuable skill. It is not just about writing code. It is about creating solutions that can improve processes, support decision making, and address practical challenges.

Towards the end of the semester, I noticed a significant difference in my confidence level. The student who struggled to write basic Python programs during the first few weeks was now able to understand larger problems and develop complete solutions. There was still a lot to learn, but the fear I initially had about programming had largely disappeared.

Looking back, I realize that Programming Fundamentals was not simply a course about Python. It was a course about problem solving, logical thinking, patience, and continuous improvement. These skills are valuable far beyond programming and will continue to help me throughout my Computer Engineering degree.

Another reason I wanted to document this experience is because this portfolio serves as a record of my learning journey. Years from now, I will be able to revisit these posts and remember the challenges, mistakes, and achievements that shaped my early university experience.

For students who are just beginning Programming Fundamentals, my advice is simple. Do not be discouraged by mistakes or slow progress. Every programmer starts as a beginner. The key is to stay consistent, keep practicing, and remain curious. Improvement happens gradually, often without you noticing it.

As I completed the Programming Fundamentals course, I felt prepared to move on to new challenges. One of those challenges arrived in the following semester through Database Systems. While programming taught me how to build solutions, databases would teach me how to store, organize, and manage the information that modern applications depend on.

That journey began with my very first Database Systems class, where I was introduced to an entirely new side of computing.

## Reflection

Programming Fundamentals was one of the most influential courses of my first year. It introduced me to programming, but more importantly, it changed the way I think and solve problems. The course taught me persistence, logical reasoning, and the value of continuous learning.

Most importantly, it showed me that growth comes from practice and experience rather than perfection. Every challenge, mistake, and project contributed to my development as a Computer Engineering student, and those lessons will continue to guide me throughout my academic and professional journey.
